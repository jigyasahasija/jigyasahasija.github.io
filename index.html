Reproducible Benchmarking for Capacitated Multi-Agent Pickup and Delivery

Current research · RAIL Lab · Georgia Tech

I am building a reproducible framework for capacitated many-to-many multi-agent pickup and delivery (MAPD) in payload-constrained logistics environments, with identical task traces, maps, starting states, horizons, and seeds for fair comparison.

Benchmarking is the first phase, not the endpoint: a frozen runnable baseline, explicit Task, Agent, ScheduleEvent, and WorldState models, replayable event logs, and invariant checks for capacity, ordering, assignment, conflicts, deadlines, and executed cost.

After validation, I will develop and evaluate a many-to-many MAPD algorithm. Initial work uses TPMC and RMCA under capacity 1, with A* estimates and reservation-table space-time A*; later experiments will study higher-capacity insertion, allocation, routing, throughput, planning time, and execution cost.

Research methods

  • Frozen runnable repository baseline
  • Replayable task traces and identical seeds
  • Invariant-driven validation
  • A* shortest-path estimates
  • Reservation-table space-time A*
  • TPMC and RMCA initial baselines
  • Independent event-log replay
  • Multi-seed evaluation
  • Algorithm development after benchmark validation

FPGA Implementation of Residue Number System based Digital Differentiator

Prior Research · Advised by Prof. Tarun Kumar Rawat · Netaji Subhas Institute of Technology

  • The Problem: Traditional binary arithmetic introduces severe computational carry-propagation delays and power overheads when executing high-frequency digital signal processing on resource-constrained or edge devices.
  • The Approach: Evaluated alternative mathematical frameworks by designing and implementing a full-band Digital Differentiator using a three-moduli Residue Number System (RNS). Built the underlying hardware description architectures in Verilog HDL and simulated execution workflows on an Artix-7 FPGA target using the Xilinx Vivado design suite.
  • The Outcome: Demonstrated that custom-tailored RNS modular structures compress arithmetic bottlenecks and decrease total processing time for edge digital systems compared to standard infinite impulse response configurations.

Conference Presentation: “FPGA Implementation of Residue Number System based Digital Differentiator” — peer-reviewed and presented at The International Conference on Signal Processing, VLSI and Communication Engineering (ICSPVCE), Delhi Technological University.
